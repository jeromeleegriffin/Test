GRIFFIN HOUSE OF ROOKS
THE FINAL INTELLIGENCE VERDICT
Independent research evaluation | Owner: Jerome Griffin | 2026-10-11

VERDICT: A. X1 SHOULD BECOME THE DEFAULT MAIN BRAIN.

If this were my game, I would choose the original frozen CHALLENGER-X1 today,
retain Sol7 as the switchable backup, and stop developing challengers for this
decision. X1's advantage survives a more relevant test: changing just one seat,
keeping the other three players fixed, and playing complete games with changing
scores. This strengthens the earlier recommendation from leading candidate to
recommended default foundation. It does not certify an untested browser port
or establish that every future opponent, human partnership or rule option is solved.
No integration, deployment, production, persona, pointer, repository or Android
change was performed by this assignment.

THE ONE EXPERIMENT

Matched single-seat substitution in full games to 500, across four frozen table
environments. Compare X1+the same partner against Sol7+that same partner, with
the same two opponents, same deck stream, same physical seat and initial dealer.
Each independent stream was repeated in four seat rotations. The first three
environments contain NO Sol7 outside the tested seat, directly challenging the
idea that X1 only works against the policy used in its rollouts. The fourth puts
it with an X1 teammate against two X1 opponents, testing a likely future table.

80 independently seeded streams, 20 per environment; four rotations per stream;
two engines per rotation. This is 320 matched game comparisons and 640 games,
NOT 640 independent observations and NOT 3,181 independent deals. Statistical
uncertainty uses 80 whole-stream clusters, stratified by the four environments.

Why this adds more than another ordinary tournament: previous tests already
established isolated-hand strength. They did not establish the marginal value
of installing one engine into a varied table while the actual game score and
auction incentives evolve. This test evaluates the game-winning objective,
partner compatibility with different existing policies, and rollout mismatch
together through one predeclared causal comparison. No human-like policy was
invented, and these bot partners are not described as real humans.

INFORMATION, FAIRNESS AND AUDIT

Both candidates received only their own cards, public state and their own
buried nest when bidder. Neither received its real teammate's hand, opposing
hands, the actual shuffle seed, future cards or lineup identities. EPA was OFF.
The same strict detached-input Adapter enforced this boundary; all 12 forbidden
field probes were rejected. Native search receives sampled worlds, not the
real unseen allocation. Enforcement is API/source based, not an adversarial
operating-system sandbox.

Official cards were generated after the design/code freeze. All 1,200 scheduled
deck hashes are distinct, with no overlap against 12,650 recoverable prior
distinct deals, or the four new preflight control deals. This cannot certify
absence from unavailable historical logs; those were not fabricated.

Every seat/dealer/environment/engine cell has five game executions. All 320
first-hand auctions, trump selections and discards are identical between arms.
Among shared later hand numbers, 860 preplay sequences stayed identical and 317
differed. Those later differences follow changed cumulative scores and the
SAME score-aware bidding code, not a stronger auction added to X1.

The independent verifier reconstructed EVERY official auction, kept/buried
card, play order, ownership, follow-suit obligation, trick winner, counter,
contract and score. All 46 cards and all 200 points accounted for each hand.
A separate match audit reconstructed cumulative scores, stopping, rotations
and pair membership. Sixteen prespecified hands rerun in a fresh process
reproduced every action, result and deterministic digest. The entire experiment
was executed once; auditing all logs is not described as replaying all games.

CHALLENGING THE CONCLUSION AND STRATEGIC LIMITS

Implementation error: original-byte and saved-action checks passed, alongside
all-hand independent rule/score reconstruction and fresh-process replay.
Seat/deal bias: every seat/dealer cell balanced; identical deck schedules within
pairs; bootstrap resamples complete four-rotation clusters.
Information bias: equal legal views, no EPA, hidden-field rejection.
Sol7 rollout exploitation: gain persists with Meridian/Extreme/Corvus outside
the tested seat and at the X1-populated table. This weakens that explanation;
it does not establish superiority to every unseen opponent.
Auction/discard bias: algorithms shared and unchanged; first-hand parity is
exact. Full-game effects include downstream score-induced auction changes.
Statistical artifact: one frozen primary endpoint, fixed sample, stratified
paired intervals, subgroup multiplicity adjustment, zero excluded failures.

Inconvenient evidence is retained. Sol7 won 8 paired games X1 lost: 2 with
Extreme, 1 with Meridian, 1 with Corvus and 4 at the X1 table. One Extreme case
(block 13, seat 3) ended with X1's team scoring 0 against 565; the corresponding Sol7 team
won 615 against 450. Outcomes alone do not identify which single move was a mistake.
All eight full-game records and their complete action logs remain available.

X1 is not a wholesale replacement of everything Sol7 learned. It inherits the
exact Sol7 auction, trump choice and discard policy. Its gain comes from search
over card-play decisions. Important weaknesses remain:
- It predicts Sol7 continuation for every simulated player, including its own
  future moves. That is an imperfect model of X1, other bots and people.
- Hidden-hand allocation uses remaining cards and observed voids, but not a
  calibrated likelihood model of bidding, trump choice or play intentions.
- The search utility maximizes final HAND score margin, not probability of
  winning the whole MATCH. Cumulative score affects inherited bidding but is
  absent from the native card-play utility. Winning this test does not remove
  that architectural limitation.
- Approximate sampling and action selection are not a solved Rook strategy.
- Human teammates were not tested. Helpful lead transfers and counter feeds
  may emerge from scoring search; understanding a person's intent is unproven.

Descriptive contract outcomes support the result: X1-arm teams made 777/853
contracts (91.0903%); Sol7-arm teams made 731/859 (85.09895%). Opponents made
604/734 against the X1 arm and 636/735 against the Sol7 arm. These aggregates
have different visited states and stopping times; they are not another paired
causal endpoint. No isolated tactical example is presented as proof of optimality.

EPA IMPLICATIONS

Choose X1 as the EPA foundation as well, but do not bundle EPA into the claim
that frozen X1 won this experiment. This experiment used NO privileged cards.
The reviewed single-partner EPA archives suggest improved team margins, not
proven human partnership satisfaction. Their 10,000-hand validation's +7.44
point mean is reproducible from its result rows, but its 5,000 rows contain
only 4,250 distinct shuffled deals: 1,250 seeds per proxy with a 1,000-seed stride
caused overlap. The focal bot always occupied the dealer seat. The published
independent-deal count and coverage were overstated. A descriptive seed-cluster
robust interval remains positive (about +5.47 to +9.41); this does not repair
the design limits or turn proxies into people.

The EPA duel's 4,472/177/351 paired result and +53.37 margin are reproducible
from its rows. It compared X1's partner-constrained sampling with a modified
Sol7 unseen-card rule; all four bots received partner-hand knowledge. That is
an EPA-IMPLEMENTATION comparison, not an equal frozen-engine comparison, and
not the owner's intended sole-human deployment situation. It is not the basis
for this verdict. Full EPA hand traces were not available in those archives,
so their every-play legality cannot be re-audited to the same standard here.

For the intended product: only the bot partnering the sole human in an offline
game may use that human's remaining cards. Opponents get no such access, and
human multiplayer must have EPA disabled. Nothing in this research modifies or
certifies current integration enforcement. EPA human experience remains unproven.

RUNTIME AND MOBILE LIMITS

Official 640-game run: 224.915 seconds in this Linux environment.
Matched 128 nonforced decision views, 32 per environment; three repetitions each:
  X1: mean 6.2398 ms, median 4.0170 ms,
      p95 19.7616 ms, p99 25.6351 ms, max 37.5094 ms.
  Sol7: mean 0.0742 ms, p95 0.2165 ms.
X1 cost about 84.1 times as much on these identical views.
It chose differently from Sol7 on 50/128 views. Views follow a fixed first 32
nonforced calls per environment; they are not a representative phone sample.

Across actual focal decisions, X1 averaged 4.9509 ms with p95 18.5932 ms, but the
largest observed call was 391.5677 ms. Scheduling may contribute; this is not a
proven worst-case bound. Native server timing is NOT Android timing. The
preserved Python/Linux implementation is not a browser-ready installation.
The separately active browser integration was neither touched nor benchmarked.
Responsive phone behavior remains an integration acceptance issue; the strength
verdict is not a reason to assert that an unmeasured port is fast enough.

ENGINEERING JUDGMENT

Make frozen X1 the default foundational intelligence. Preserve Sol7 unchanged
as the fallback and acknowledge that X1 builds on its bidding and discard work.
The combined evidence now supports a real match-winning improvement across
varied tables, not just a repeated victory over one opponent. This final test
strengthens, rather than reverses, the previous X1 recommendation. No additional
challenger-development project is justified by the evidence needed for this
choice. Phone integration quality and human EPA experience are separate product
questions, not reasons to conceal the clear engine-strength result.
